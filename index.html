Synthetic Ornithology — Evaluation Samples

This site gathers audio examples generated by EAGLE (Environmental Audio Generation for Localised Ecologies), presented as part of the research project Synthetic Ornithology. The examples support reflective listening to the model’s outputs, including their fidelity, ambiguity, and limits.

Synthetic Ornithology uses worldbuilding that aligns closely with existing Australian ecological sound environments. This makes the generated samples complex to evaluate: listeners with knowledge of these environments may compare them against specific sonic memories or expectations. The aim of these examples is not to demonstrate the accurate simulation of particular places, but to show how EAGLE renders a consistent sonic world, and where that rendering becomes unstable.

  • Comparisons — Side-by-side comparisons of model outputs and source recordings, used to compare acoustic features and perceptual fidelity.
  • Notable Cases — Moments of compelling ambiguity or emergent behaviour that create opportunities for audience engagement and reveal the boundary between adherence to the world schema and speculative variation.
  • Failure Modes — Outputs that expose the model’s fragility, including audible artefacts, unstable correspondences, and moments of misalignment with the schema.

Listening notes

  • Context matters. These examples are conditioned by place, time, and weather. The clip labels describe the conditioning values used to generate or contextualise each sample.
  • Plausibility is not prediction. Some outputs may sound convincing without functioning as accurate ecological simulations. Synthetic Ornithology uses the rhetoric of simulation as part of a speculative creative framework, not as a claim to predictive ecological modelling.
  • Misalignment is not always failure. Within the creative work, some degrees of instability contribute positively by producing ambiguity, surprise, or moments where the limits of the system become audible.

About

Synthetic Ornithology treats machine learning as a compositional process in which conditioning data, dataset structure, and model behaviour together produce a rendered world. The examples here illustrate three facets of that process: correspondence, emergence, and breakdown. Together, these samples show how EAGLE can produce outputs that sustain a perceptually plausible sense of worldhood, while also making visible the limits of that world’s stability.
